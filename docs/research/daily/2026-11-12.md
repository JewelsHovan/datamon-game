## G-01: Game-loop engineering for browser fighters

### Findings
- Polling `navigator.getGamepads()` in requestAnimationFrame runs "typically around 60Hz", far below the OS poll rate ("eg., 250Hz"). "The average added input delay is approximately half the polling interval", about 8.33 ms at 60 Hz [primary] (https://microsoftedge.github.io/MSEdgeExplainers/GamepadEventDrivenInputAPI/explainer.html).
- The same explainer says "input changes can be missed between polling intervals" and proposes a `gamepadrawinputchanged` event on `window`, one per HID report, with `buttonsPressed` and `buttonsReleased`. Polling stays; Firefox and Safari show "No Signal"; no shipped browser version or flag is stated [primary] (same URL).
- Chromium has a 2025 Intent to Prototype for an event-driven gamepad model [secondary] (https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/5IpJBqhqrMg).
- Chrome's internal gamepad poll is 250 Hz (4 ms), per a 2021 W3C list message; the old 16 ms figure is outdated [secondary] (https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-webapps-github/2021Aug/0513.html).
- Checking key state once per frame misses a press and release that both happen between two checks; handling the event queue or latching "pressed since last poll" fixes it. Consistent across SDL, LÖVE and MonoGame forums [secondary] (https://discourse.libsdl.org/t/polling-events-vs-get-keyboardstate/39050, https://love2d.org/forums/viewtopic.php?p=197372).
- Measured with a phototransistor on a 60 Hz display, Chrome on Windows took one to two frames from drawing in a rAF callback to light on screen ("presentation delay varied between one and two frames"). No data on the `desynchronized` hint or fullscreen [primary] (https://aforren1.github.io/chrome-lag-test/index.md.html, 2022).
- The canvas `desynchronized` context attribute bypasses the compositor queue to cut latency; it can't be changed after the first `getContext()`, and clearing the visible canvas can flicker, so draw offscreen and copy [secondary] (https://developer.chrome.com/blog/desynchronized).
- A measured vsync delta "is never exactly equal to the vsync interval", which causes "a heartbeat like stutter of a long delta followed by a short delta". Fix: round the delta to the refresh interval and keep the difference in a buffer that is "subtracted off the next delta". Article is DirectX, 2012, no tolerance values [primary] (https://frankforce.com/frame-rate-delta-buffering/).
- Tyler Glaiel's "time snapping" rounds the delta to 1/60 (and multiples) when it is within about 0.0002 s, and calls the thresholds untuned [secondary: Medium returned 403] (https://medium.com/@tglaiel/how-to-make-your-game-run-at-60fps-24c61210fe75).
- Without a fix, an accumulator's remainder drifts: about 1.66 ms builds up over roughly 1000 frames, then one frame runs two steps [secondary, forum] (https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/695499-am-i-understanding-fixed-timestep-games-loop-wrong/5372763/).
- Render interpolation "always lags one tick behind"; the alternative is a throwaway render-only state stepped by the leftover time, which the author flags as a determinism risk [primary] (https://jakubtomsu.github.io/posts/fixed_timestep_without_interpolation, 2024). This supports the design's no-interpolation rule; no addition.

### Candidate additions
- A-012 (new): latch presses between samples so a short tap or a press during a multi-step catch-up is never lost.
- A-013 (new): vsync-aware accumulator that snaps near-whole-frame deltas and carries the remainder, plus a pacing counter in the F1 overlay.
- A-014 (new): input-latency budget and an opt-in `desynchronized` canvas, shown in the F1 overlay.
- Dropped: `gamepadrawinputchanged` as its own addition. No browser ships it; folded into A-012 as a feature-detected extra.

### G-19: Frame pacing for a 60 Hz no-interpolation renderer on high-refresh displays
#### Findings
- RetroArch's guide: uneven refresh rates "like 75/90/144/165 Hz are only able to maintain smooth frame pacing" with VRR active. For 120 and 240 Hz, use a swap interval of the even multiple ("120Hz/2 = 60Hz"). Outside the timing skew, "the display repeats or skips frames to make up the difference, which shows as judder" [primary] (https://docs.libretro.com/guides/optimal-vsync).
- Same guide: within a 0.05 skew (default), content runs at the display rate instead and "every frame is shown once and scrolling is smooth"; a 57.5 Hz arcade game runs at 60 [primary] (same URL).
- Chrome runs requestAnimationFrame at the refresh rate of the monitor holding most of the window, with no built-in 60 fps cap; the old `--limit-fps` flag no longer works [secondary] (https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/chromium-html5/c/qLxS7RmTM8w, https://forums.blurbusters.com/viewtopic.php?p=25739).
- A WICG proposal to "Allow developers to indicate their ideal frame rate" names the gap (some 120 Hz devices "still run rAF callbacks at 60 Hz for compatibility reasons"; games can't reliably run at a fixed sub-rate) but had no API and no vendor response as of the Feb 2024 archive [primary] (https://discourse.wicg.io/t/proposal-allow-developers-to-indicate-their-ideal-frame-rate/4422).
- Mark Rejhon (Blur Busters) asked the W3C web-perf group in Sept 2024 whether there is "movement to adding variable refresh rate support" for animations; no answer in the thread [primary] (https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-web-perf/2024Sep/0005.html). Browsers schedule rAF at a fixed display rate, so VRR does not rescue 60 Hz content on a 144 Hz panel [secondary] (same Blur Busters thread).
- Safari reportedly delivers rAF at 60 Hz where Chrome gives 120 Hz on the same device [secondary] (https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/mobile-browsers-and-the-60fps-cap/47582).
- Conclusion for this game: at 60/120/240 Hz every sim frame can be shown for an equal number of refreshes; at 144/165 Hz a 2-3 refresh cadence (judder) is unavoidable without interpolation, which the design rules out. Nothing found contradicts the no-interpolation rule; the cost is a known, reportable limitation.
#### Candidate additions
- A-016 (new): display-rate report in the F1 overlay, plus redraw only when a new sim frame exists.
- A-013 (updated): snap deltas to the measured display interval, not just 1/60 s, so 120 Hz alternates 0-1-0-1 steps cleanly.
- Dropped: a "sync to exact content rate" mode. No browser API exists for VRR or a requested frame rate.

### G-02: Frame-data conventions for simple-input fighters
#### Findings
- Granblue Fantasy Versus: Rising, post-beta adjustments: throw break window reduced; throw breaks work with any attack button; a late break still takes reduced damage and gives "an advantage to the attacker and a chance to continue applying pressure" [primary] (https://gematsu.com/2023/10/granblue-fantasy-versus-rising-details-major-adjustments-to-mechanics-in-response-to-first-open-beta-test).
- Same source: technical inputs get +10% damage rather than a penalty on simple inputs, "an advantage to technical inputs instead of a disadvantage to simple inputs"; Fukuhara: "this is a game we want new players to enjoy too" [primary] (same URL). No bearing: this game has no motion inputs.
- SF6 throw escape window lasts until the 9th frame of being thrown, and an escape fails if another action input happens in that window (anti option-select) [secondary] (https://srk.shib.live/w/Street_Fighter_6/Defense, snippet). Strike beats throw on the same frame [secondary] (same).
- Infil on SF6's beta: tech window "seems to be rather large", making delayed tech strong; teching "sends you almost full screen, so your pressure is over" [primary] (https://words.infil.net/w03-sf6beta-p4.html). No frame numbers.
- Sakurai (Famitsu 490-491, fan translation): hitstop depends on damage plus per-attack modifiers (more for electric, less for projectiles); there is a cap "on the maximum amount of time characters can be in freeze frames"; both fighters freeze "for the exact same amount of time"; the hit fighter vibrates (side to side on the ground, up and down in the air) while hurtboxes stay still [primary] (https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/).
- His "Eight Hit Stop Techniques" video adds gradually reducing the shake, interpolating into the damage pose, keeping the attacker moving slightly, and scaling shake with camera distance [secondary] (https://nintendowire.com/news/2022/12/12/this-week-in-sakurai-12-5-12-11-fine-tuning-hit-stop-and-cheating-the-system/, video https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tycbMSjDDLg not watched).
- Capcom's SF Seminar column presents 8/12/16 frames as a hypothetical example of one game's light/medium/heavy hitstop, not SFV's own values [secondary: search summary; page 503 again] (https://game.capcom.com/cfn/sfv/column/131545?lang=en). A later column says longer hitstop makes heavies easier to cancel [secondary] (https://game.capcom.com/cfn/sfv/column/132455?lang=en, 503).
#### Candidate additions
- A-004 (updated): any attack button breaks a throw; strike beats throw on the same frame; tech pushes fighters apart; GBVS evidence is primary.
- A-003 (updated): principle now primary (Sakurai); add a hitstop cap and equal freeze for both fighters; 8/12/16 relabeled as an example.
- A-017 (new): hitstop shake on the hit fighter, render-only, decaying, axis by grounded or airborne.
- Dropped: technical-input damage bonus (no motion inputs in this design).
