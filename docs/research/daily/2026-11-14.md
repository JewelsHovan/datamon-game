## G-23: Explaining a loss to newcomers

### Findings
- Celia Wagar suggests "more relevant post-match statistics, like how many overheads you blocked, how many times you successfully anti-aired", and "Match analysis based on simple statistical points, keep track of what moves specifically you get hit by". The analysis should "suggest moves that the playerbase commonly interrupts or punishes that move with". She also lists "Built in replay recording", citing Smash Ultimate [primary] (https://critpoints.net/2021/05/07/how-fighting-games-can-retain-players/).
- Tekken 8 My Replay & Tips (AUTOMATON, 2024-01-31): during replay playback it shows advice in real time. It points out punishes the player missed (確定反撃), throw escapes, and recommended combos that do more damage than the one used. At any point the player can take over and replay the action for 10 seconds. Players praised it on social media as accessible and good for beginners [primary] (https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/20240131-280770/).
- GamingBolt, 2023-12-12: Kohei Ikeda says the goal is to avoid "a lot of jargon and technical data". Jun Yasuda: "the computer tells you exactly what you're doing wrong, and then it allows you to practice on the spot." It doesn't say which moments are flagged [primary] (https://gamingbolt.com/tekken-8-overhauls-my-replay-and-tips-allows-for-real-time-practice/amp).
- The feature first appeared in Tekken 7 Season 2 [secondary] (https://www.oneesports.gg/tekken/tekken-8-replays-and-tips/). Prima Games' write-up returned 403.
- Smash results screens show KOs, Falls, SDs, Damage Given or Taken and Peak Damage. Per-character Battle Data has reportedly informed Ultimate's balance patches [secondary] (https://www.ssbwiki.com/Results, https://www.ssbwiki.com/Battle_Data).
- A defeat screen that shows only negative stats may push players to tilt or quit [secondary, low-quality source] (https://mail.infosecinstitute.com/league-defeat-screen-1165466103.html). The claim isn't fighting-game specific, so it is used only as a framing caution.

### Candidate additions
- A-021 (new): the results screen shows two or three positive, specific stats per player and the move that hit them most, with a one-line answer to it.
- A-020 (new): a "What happened?" replay of the match from the input log that pauses on missed punishes and unescaped throws, with a 10-second take-over.
- A-009 (updated): the K.O. replay now has evidence (Wagar's built-in replays) and shares its replay plumbing with A-020. No rescore.
- Dropped: a ghost AI trained on the player's own matches (Tekken 8 Super Ghost). This needs learning or recording infrastructure far past step 8's CPU, and couch players rarely rematch themselves. G-10 can look at it.

## G-09: Meter design

### Findings
- Rival Schools developers (Capcom, 1998 interview, translated): early meter gain gave "up to 9 meters with just a bit of fighting", which was "too far!", so it was cut to 3. Itsuno: "we wanted to make it so that any time you felt like using a Team-Up, you'd have around 3 or so meters. That's why we buffed up the meter gain." The reason was that "Those Team-Ups are what separate Rival Schools from other games." He also wanted players to save them for the finishing blow, and said seeing them too often gets tiring [primary] (https://itsfantastic.moe/rival-schools-developer-interview/).
- Yoshinori Ono on SF4's Ultra meter, which fills from damage taken: its real value was that "it's something there that can prevent a perfect from happening too", and "I think we've been very successful in avoiding perfect KOs on beginning players", so "people feel like they've at least accomplished something". SFV's V-System "gives you an opportunity to make a comeback, but it also gives you a chance to keep from being perfect KO'd" [primary] (https://gamesbeat.com/street-fighter-v-round-3-yoshinori-ono-dishes-on-the-comeback-mechanic/). The interviewer criticized Ultra as a comeback mechanic (same URL).
- Mortal Kombat 1: the defender gains more meter from being hit than the attacker gains from hitting, and gains nothing from blocking [secondary] (https://srk.shib.live/w/Mortal_Kombat_1/Gauges, snippet; bot-blocked).
- Samurai Shodown's meter filled from damage taken; Super Turbo popularized gain from damage dealt [secondary] (https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Super_Meter).
- Design forum criticism: very aggressive meter gain can make a round turn into "stop-and-fish" play two-thirds of the way through [secondary] (https://selectbutton.net/t/fight-man-game-design-chat-resource-management/334).
- SF6's single Drive gauge: not found. No Nakayama quote on why it's one gauge. The GamesRadar interview body was truncated on fetch.

### Candidate additions
- A-018 (updated): meter also builds from taking damage, at a higher rate than from dealing it, after Ono's beginner rationale. A tuning target (about one super per player per round, measured headlessly) follows Rival Schools' "too far" lesson. Rescored 16 to 17.
- Dropped: Ultra-style separate revenge meter. A second gauge adds HUD and rules for newcomers, and the design has one super meter. The damage-taken part folds into A-018.
- A-011 (no change): no primary evidence on SF6's one-gauge design was found.
